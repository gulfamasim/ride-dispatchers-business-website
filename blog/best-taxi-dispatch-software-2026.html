Platforms

Best taxi dispatch software in 2026

We work inside five dispatch platforms every single day. Here is the honest read on which one suits which kind of fleet.

Ride Dispatchers Operations Team 28 July 2026 9 min read

Most "best taxi dispatch software" lists are written by people who have never allocated a job at 2am on a Saturday. Ours is not. Our agents work inside these platforms on live fleet accounts every day, across the UK and Ireland, so this comparison is written from the dispatcher's chair rather than the vendor's brochure.

Who this guide is for

This is aimed at small and mid-size taxi and private-hire operators — roughly 10 to 250 vehicles. If you run 2,000 cars across multiple cities, your procurement process will be driven by integration requirements we are not going to cover here. If you run four cars, most of this is overkill and you should pick the cheapest thing with a decent driver app.

What actually matters when you run the desk

Feature grids are mostly noise. After thousands of hours inside these systems, the things that genuinely change your day are:

  • Time to allocate a job. How many clicks and keystrokes between answering the phone and the driver having the booking. On a busy Friday this is the whole game.
  • How well it handles the messy cases. Multi-stop, account jobs, pre-bookings, waiting time, a driver dropping a live job. Every platform does the happy path fine.
  • Access control. Can you issue named accounts with narrow permissions and revoke them instantly? This matters enormously if anyone outside your office touches the system.
  • Whether it is truly cloud. A browser-based system means a dispatcher can work from anywhere. A "cloud" system that still needs a desktop client or a VPN does not.
  • Reporting you would actually act on. Answer time, abandonment, conversion. Not a wall of call counts.
  • What support looks like at 1am. Not the SLA on the contract — what actually happens.

Cabica — our recommendation for most fleets

Preferred platform

Why we point fleets at Cabica. It is a cloud taxi dispatch and booking platform, and being genuinely browser-based changes what an outsourced desk can do. Named agent accounts, precise permissions, instant revocation, no VPN into your office and no software to install on our floor. Fleets already running Cabica are consistently the quickest to go live with us.

Cabica is where we send operators who are choosing fresh or are ready to move, and the reason is boringly practical rather than ideological.

It is genuinely browser-native. That sounds like a checkbox until you try to staff a desk remotely, at which point it becomes the whole difference. Our agents in Lahore log in through named accounts the fleet owner creates, with permissions scoped to exactly what they need. When a rota changes, access changes in seconds. When an agent rolls off the account, the fleet revokes them directly. Nobody installs anything, nobody opens a VPN tunnel into the operator's office, and there is no shared login floating around — which is the single most common security failure we see on other platforms.

Day to day, the booking-to-allocation flow is short, the interface holds up on a small screen, and the driver and passenger apps come from the same stack, so the pieces behave consistently. Reporting covers the metrics that map to revenue rather than vanity call volumes.

Best for: fleets from around 10 to 150 vehicles, anyone planning to outsource or remote-staff any part of the desk, and operators who want a modern setup without an enterprise procurement cycle.

Worth knowing: it is a newer name than Autocab or iCabbi. If your buying process weights vendor age heavily, factor that in — though for most owner-operated fleets that matters far less than day-to-day usability.

Autocab — the established incumbent

Autocab is the most widely deployed system in the UK and has the ecosystem to match: the iGo network, deep integrations, and account-management infrastructure built for large operators.

Our agents work on it constantly and it is capable. For a large fleet with corporate accounts, complex tariffs and existing integrations, it is a legitimately strong choice, and the network effects of iGo are real if you want overflow work from other operators.

Best for: established fleets over roughly 100 vehicles, heavy corporate account work, and operators who want the network.

Worth knowing: it carries the weight of a mature product. Onboarding a new dispatcher takes longer than on lighter systems, and commercial terms tend to suit larger operators better than small ones.

iCabbi — strong on the passenger side

iCabbi is cloud-based and has invested heavily in the rider experience and automation — app booking, automated dispatch logic, and a slick passenger journey. Fleets competing directly with ride-hailing apps on customer experience tend to like it.

From the desk, the automation is genuinely useful: it takes routine allocations off the dispatcher so the human attention goes to exceptions. That is the right design principle.

Best for: fleets where app bookings are a large and growing share of volume, and operators who want automation to reduce dispatcher headcount.

Worth knowing: when automated dispatch does something unexpected, unpicking why takes more digging than on a simpler system. Your team needs to understand the rules, not just trust them.

Cordic — solid, traditional, dependable

Cordic has been serving UK operators for a long time and has a loyal base for good reason: it is stable, it handles complex tariff and account structures properly, and it does not break.

Best for: established fleets with complicated account and tariff requirements who value stability over a modern interface.

Worth knowing: the interface shows its age, and remote or browser-based working is less natural than on Cabica or iCabbi. If outsourcing part of your desk is on the roadmap, check that path carefully before committing.

TaxiCaller — the budget entry point

TaxiCaller is cloud-based, inexpensive and quick to set up. For a small fleet moving off paper, a spreadsheet or a WhatsApp group, it is a sensible first system and the price is hard to argue with.

Best for: fleets under about 25 vehicles, and operators buying their first proper dispatch system.

Worth knowing: fleets tend to outgrow it. Once you are running serious account work or high pre-booking volume, the ceiling arrives.

If you are choosing today and you are a small or mid-size fleet, start with Cabica. If you are already on Autocab, iCabbi or Cordic and it works, the migration cost is rarely worth paying for its own sake — we run desks on all of them without complaint.

One closing note that costs nothing: whichever platform you pick, insist on named user accounts from day one. Shared logins are the root of nearly every access-control problem we encounter, and they make outsourcing any part of your desk considerably harder later.
